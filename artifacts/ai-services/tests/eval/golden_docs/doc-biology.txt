Photosynthesis overview
Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose.

Chlorophyll pigments
Chlorophyll a and chlorophyll b absorb red and blue light in the thylakoid membranes.

Light reactions
The light dependent reactions split water, release oxygen, and produce ATP and NADPH.

Calvin cycle
The Calvin cycle fixes carbon dioxide into sugar in the stroma using ATP and NADPH.

Stomata gas exchange
Stomata control carbon dioxide intake and water loss from leaves.

Factors affecting rate
Light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, and temperature limit the rate of photosynthesis.

C4 plants
C4 plants such as maize concentrate carbon dioxide to reduce photorespiration in hot climates.

CAM plants
CAM plants open stomata at night and store carbon dioxide as malic acid.

Chloroplast structure
Chloroplasts contain thylakoids stacked into grana surrounded by stroma fluid.

Photosystems
Photosystem II and photosystem I work in series in the electron transport chain.

Photorespiration
Photorespiration occurs when rubisco binds oxygen instead of carbon dioxide, wasting energy.

Rubisco enzyme
Rubisco is the most abundant enzyme on Earth and catalyzes carbon fixation.
